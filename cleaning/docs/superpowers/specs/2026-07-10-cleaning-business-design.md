# Home Cleaning Business — Design & Launch Plan

**Date:** 2026-07-10
**Model:** Recurring residential cleaning; owner runs marketing/booking/ops; independent 1099 subcontractor teams fulfill jobs
**Goal:** $8–15k/mo net profit within 12–18 months
**Startup budget:** $2,000–5,000
**Market:** US metro/suburb (state TBD — confirm before filing; see §2 caveat)

---

## 1. Business model and unit economics

Customers book and pay the company. Independent cleaning teams fulfill jobs for 55–60% of the job fee. The company keeps 40–45% gross margin; after marketing, software, insurance, and payment fees (~10–12% of revenue), net margin lands around 30–33%.

**Unit economics (average job $150, biweekly customer):**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Average job price | $150 |
| Team payout (57.5% avg) | $86 |
| Gross margin per job | $64 (42.5%) |
| Card fees (~2.9%) | $4.35 |
| Biweekly customer annual revenue | ~$3,900 |
| Biweekly customer annual gross profit | ~$1,660 |
| Max acceptable CAC | $75 (target < $60) |

**Path to goal:**

| Net profit | Revenue | Recurring customers | Teams |
|---|---|---|---|
| $5k/mo | ~$16k/mo | ~55 | 2–3 |
| $10k/mo | ~$30k/mo | ~100–110 | 5–6 |
| $15k/mo | ~$45k/mo | ~160 | 8–9 |

## 2. Worker classification guardrails (critical)

The entire model depends on teams being legitimately independent contractors:

- Teams provide their own supplies, equipment, and methods; they may refuse any job
- Payment is per job (flat split), never hourly; W-9 on file; signed subcontractor agreement before first job
- Teams are encouraged/required to carry their own general liability insurance (collect COI); company carries its own GL policy + janitorial bond as the brand
- No training manuals, no supervision, no required uniforms/methods. Quality is enforced only through customer ratings, a reclean guarantee, and routing future jobs to higher-rated teams
- File 1099-NEC for any team paid $600+/year

**Caveat:** In ABC-test states (CA, MA, NJ, IL, and others trending that way), this model is legally fragile — a cleaner working under your brand may be presumed an employee. **Confirm operating state before forming the entity.** If in an ABC state, revisit the hybrid (subs → W-2) model.

## 3. Pricing

Flat-rate by home size, quoted instantly online (no in-home estimates):

| Home | Standard | Deep clean (+50%) | Move-in/out (+75%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1bd/1ba | $110 | $165 | $195 |
| 2bd/2ba | $150 | $225 | $260 |
| 3bd/2ba | $185 | $280 | $325 |
| 4bd/3ba | $230 | $345 | $400 |

Frequency discounts off standard rate: weekly −20%, biweekly −15%, monthly −10%. First clean is always priced as a deep clean. Adjust ±15% after checking 3–5 local competitors' quotes.

## 4. Setup costs (~$1,200–1,800)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| LLC filing + EIN + registered agent | $100–400 (state-dependent) |
| General liability insurance + janitorial bond | $700–1,000/yr |
| Booking software (BookingKoala or Launch27) | $60–130/mo |
| Domain + website (self-built) | ~$50 |
| Stripe | pay-as-you-go (2.9% + 30¢) |
| Background checks for teams | ~$30–50 each |

Remaining budget ($1,500–3,000) goes to customer acquisition (§6).

## 5. Team recruiting

Source existing independent cleaners (solo operators and duos who already clean but hate marketing):

- Local Facebook groups (search "[city] cleaning", "[city] house cleaners")
- Craigslist gigs/services sections
- Referrals from janitorial supply stores
- Later: referrals from existing teams (pay a bonus)

Vetting: background check, 2 references, 1–2 paid trial cleans at a known home. Sign subcontractor agreement + W-9, collect COI. **Always keep a bench:** recruit one more team than currently needed — team churn/no-shows are the #1 operational risk.

Pitch to teams: "Steady, pre-sold jobs on your schedule, no marketing, paid weekly, keep 55–60%."

## 6. Customer acquisition (budget $1,500–3,000)

Priority order:

1. **Google Business Profile** — free; get 10 reviews fast (first customers, friends' cleans)
2. **Google Local Services Ads** — pay-per-lead (~$20–35/lead residential cleaning), highest intent channel; requires background check + insurance verification ("Google Guaranteed")
3. **Website with instant online quote + booking** — the main conversion weapon; most local competitors require a phone call
4. **Nextdoor + local Facebook groups** — free; respond to "anyone know a cleaner?" posts
5. **Referral program** — give $25, get $25, promoted after every 5-star rating
6. **Realtor/property-manager outreach** — move-out cleans (high ticket) that convert sellers/landlords into recurring relationships

Targets: CAC < $75, 40%+ of one-time customers converted to recurring, 20+ Google reviews by month 3.

## 7. Operations

- Booking software owns scheduling, reminders, payment capture, and team job-offers
- Post-clean automated rating request to every customer
- Rating < 4: automatic reclean offer (funded from company margin) — this is the quality lever
- Route more jobs to high-rated teams; stop offering jobs to consistently low-rated teams
- Pay teams weekly via ACH; reconcile in the software
- Owner time: ~5–10 hrs/wk at launch (sales + recruiting), ~10–15 hrs/wk at scale

## 8. Milestones

| When | State |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | Entity, insurance, software, site live; 2 teams; first 5 customers |
| Month 3 | ~25 recurring customers; $7k/mo revenue; ~$2k/mo profit |
| Month 6 | ~55 recurring; $16k/mo revenue; ~$5k/mo profit; 3 teams |
| Month 12–18 | 100+ recurring; $30k+/mo revenue; **$10k+/mo profit**; 5–6 teams |

## 9. Risks and mitigations

1. **Worker misclassification** — guardrails in §2; confirm state law before filing; keep the relationship genuinely arms-length
2. **Team churn / no-shows** — bench team, weekly pay, be the best job source in town; customer-facing reschedule flow for emergencies
3. **Quality variance** — ratings + reclean guarantee + job routing; fire (stop booking) bad teams fast
4. **Seasonality/slow start** — move-out cleans and realtor work fill gaps while recurring base builds

## 10. Launch checklist

### Week 1 — Legal & financial foundation
- [ ] Confirm operating state → check contractor-classification law (ABC test?)
- [ ] File LLC, get EIN, open business bank account
- [ ] Buy general liability policy + janitorial bond (NEXT, Thimble, Hiscox — compare 3 quotes)
- [ ] Draft subcontractor agreement (attorney review or vetted template; include per-job pay, own supplies/methods, right of refusal, COI requirement, no exclusivity)

### Week 2 — Systems
- [ ] Sign up for BookingKoala or Launch27; configure flat-rate pricing table (§3)
- [ ] Connect Stripe
- [ ] Build website: instant quote widget + online booking, services, service-area pages, reviews section
- [ ] Create Google Business Profile; start Google Local Services Ads verification (takes 1–3 weeks)

### Week 3 — Teams
- [ ] Post recruiting ads (Facebook groups, Craigslist); goal: 8–10 conversations
- [ ] Vet: background checks, references, paid trial cleans
- [ ] Sign 2 teams: agreement + W-9 + COI on file
- [ ] Agree payout split and weekly ACH pay schedule

### Week 4 — First customers
- [ ] Launch LSA + $300–500 test budget
- [ ] Book friends/family/neighbors at light discount → first Google reviews
- [ ] Post in Nextdoor + local Facebook groups
- [ ] Email/visit 10 realtors and property managers re: move-out cleans
- [ ] Turn on referral program in booking software

### Months 2–6 — Grow
- [ ] Weekly: review CAC per channel, shift budget to what converts < $75
- [ ] Convert every one-time clean to recurring (offer at job completion + follow-up email)
- [ ] Recruit 1 new team per ~20 recurring customers added; keep the bench full
- [ ] Monthly: review team ratings; reroute or cut low performers
- [ ] At $16k/mo revenue: revisit whether to raise prices, add a team, or tighten service area
